A About

Five years of making other people's systems someone's responsibility.

I'm Majid Ali — a backend and cloud engineer in Karachi. I started as a full-stack developer, became a team lead, spent a year as a project manager talking to enterprise partners, and now run technical operations for a multi-tenant healthcare platform. Alongside that I've run my own consultancy since 2022.

How I got here

The through-line in my work is ownership of the unglamorous half. Most of what I've been trusted with has been the part of a system that already exists, already has users, and has started to hurt: a cloud bill that outgrew its architecture, a data warehouse that costs more than it earns, a platform that needs to move clouds without going down, a database that only one person can answer questions from.

That's shaped how I think. I'm sceptical of rewrites, biased toward migrations you can run incrementally under live traffic, and I read cost and latency graphs as architecture documents — they tell you where the design disagrees with reality more honestly than a diagram does.

The AI work grew out of the same instinct rather than being a pivot. An MCP server is an API design problem: what can this caller reach, what must it discover first, what can it never touch. A multi-agent graph is a distributed systems problem with a probabilistic component. The interesting decisions are in the boundaries, and boundaries are the thing I've spent five years on.

The year I spent as a project manager is the part of my background I'd undersell least. Coordinating enterprise integrations with banking, insurance and telecom partners taught me what a system looks like from outside the engineering org — and how much of delivery is about being legible to people who don't read code.
